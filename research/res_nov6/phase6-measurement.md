## 1 · TL;DR — What This Phase Locks

1. **The self-report gap is real and decisive for BU's architecture.** A pre-registered meta-analysis of **106 effect sizes**: self-reported media use correlates only *moderately* with logs — "rarely an accurate reflection" — and problematic-use scales are weaker still (Parry et al. 2021, *Nat Hum Behav* 5:1535–1547). A smartphone validation (Andrews et al. 2015, *PLoS ONE*, N = 23, 2 weeks): unlock-count estimates did not correlate with reality at all (r = .11, n.s.). The Watcher is therefore the only instrument BU has that measures behavior — and manual entries are a *different instrument* (self-knowledge), never a weak copy (D1).
2. **Self-trackers lapse structurally, and the Life Log is built to survive it.** Personal informatics' stage model (Li, Dey & Forlizzi 2010: barriers cascade from collection to reflection) and lived-informatics model (Epstein et al. 2015) locate failure in *burden*, not motivation. The Life Log gets a ≤30 s/day manual friction budget, gap-tolerant schema, and a gap-flow that never nags (D2).
3. **Quantification has a hidden cost BU routes around.** Six experiments (Etkin 2016, *JCR*): measurement increases doing but decreases enjoyment by undermining intrinsic motivation. The never-quantify list (D7): journal, relationships, rest, mood — experiences stay unmeasured; only countable behaviors are graded.
4. **Same-evening recall is the right horizon, and question wording follows memory science.** Retrospective judgments track peak and end, not duration (Redelmeier & Kahneman 1996, N = 154 + 133; Fredrickson & Kahneman 1993). Same-day reconstruction validates against experience sampling (Kahneman et al. 2004, N = 909; Dockray et al. 2010, r = 0.58–0.90). Evening Close asks for moments and small verified counts — never "how productive was your day" (§2.4).
5. **Predictions ship with conformal intervals and a causal-humble grammar, or not at all.** "34% chance you do this tomorrow. Here's what raises it to 71%" (v6 verbatim) is a lie in two parts — a naked point prediction and an unsupported counterfactual. The fix is mechanical: a bigram baseline wrapped in split conformal prediction (Angelopoulos & Bates 2021 — *marginal* coverage under exchangeability, *not* conditional), abstention rules, a confidence-language table (Joslyn & LeClerc 2012), and a Forge grammar where "associated with" survives and "raises it to" dies at compile time (D3/D4; Kratochwill 2013 + Bernal 2017 set the within-user causal bar).

## 2 · Science

### §2.1 · The Self-Report Gap — What USER_CONFIRMED Actually Measures

**Mechanism.** A self-report is not a noisy reading of behavior; it is a *different measurement process altogether* — a reconstruction performed by memory, filtered through self-schema (what kind of person I am), estimation heuristics (how do people like me usually spend a day?), and the demands of the moment. When the reconstruction's target is digital behavior — something the device logged perfectly — the gap between story and log can be measured directly, and it is large. Three properties recur across the validation literature: (1) *unreliable at the individual level* — the correlation between what a person reports and what their device logged is moderate at best; (2) *biased at the aggregate level* — errors are not symmetric noise; they lean on stereotypes of "typical" use; (3) *worst exactly where the stakes are highest* — instruments built to detect "problematic" use (scales, symptom questionnaires) correlate least with actual behavior.

**The evidence.** The anchor study is Parry, Davidson, Sewall, Fisher, Mieczkowski, Quintana & Przybylski (2021, *Nature Human Behaviour* 5:1535–1547, doi:10.1038/s41562-021-01117-5): a **pre-registered systematic review and meta-analysis** of studies that measured the same digital media use both ways. Based on **106 effect sizes**, the authors found self-reported media use **correlates only moderately** with logged measurements, that "self-reports were rarely an accurate reflection of logged media use," and that measures of *problematic* media use show an **even weaker** association with usage logs `[REPLICATED-class; T2, pre-registered meta; the exact pooled r and moderator table are page-level debt-listed]`. Their stated conclusion: these findings "raise concerns about the validity of findings relying solely on self-reported measures of media use."

The smartphone-specific validation: Andrews, Ellis, Shaw & Piwek (2015, *PLoS ONE* 10(10):e0139004, PMC4625000). Twenty-three Android users (from 29 recruited; 17 female, mean age 22.52) recorded two weeks of actual use via a background app while giving daily self-estimates `[T1; single study, small N]`:

| Measure | Actual (logged) | Self-estimated | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number of uses/day | **84.68** (SD 55.23) | **37.20** | t(23) = 3.93, p < .001; **r(21) = .11, p = .610 (n.s.)** |
| Duration/day | **5.05 h** (SD 2.73) | **4.12 h** | t(22) = 1.78, p = .086; moderate correlation (p = .02) |
| MPPUS questionnaire | — | — | **No significant correlation** with any logged measure |

People cannot count their own unlocks — estimates ran less than half of reality and did not rank users correctly at all — while *duration* is estimated with moderate but real accuracy. The same conclusion class holds for self-reported internet use against client-side log data (Scharkow 2016, *Communication Methods and Measures* 10:13–27, doi:10.1080/19312458.2015.1118446) `[T1; record-level this pass]`.

**The conceptual move for BU.** The gap justifies the Watcher (P1): on a Galaxy A54, UsageStats + a foreground service gives BU what no questionnaire can — the actual stream. But the gap also *elevates* the manual entry. If self-report were merely a weak copy of observation, then BU's four grades would be a ladder of decreasing quality and the app should always prefer OBSERVED. The evidence says otherwise: a self-report is the only instrument that measures *the user's own model of their life* — and that model is simultaneously (a) load-bearing for self-regulation (people act on their story, not on their logs) and (b) demonstrably unreliable as a behavior record. So BU never averages the instruments, never lets one "correct" the other silently, and never treats agreement or disagreement between them as an error. The disagreement is itself the most interesting data point the app can surface — the moment a user's story and their record diverge is exactly where self-knowledge can grow, and it belongs to the user, not to a prompt.

**What this means for BU.**

- `[P1]` **Watcher as the behavior instrument** (Phase 4 D1's matrix is the schema; this phase adds the evidentiary grounding): drift minutes, app-class counts, seal events are OBSERVED and carry no self-report fallback. When the Watcher is suspended (usage access off, battery kill), BU records the gap — it does not estimate over it.
- `[P2]` **Manual Life Log entries are USER_CONFIRMED and stay USER_CONFIRMED** — no "verified by context" upgrade path that silently re-grades them (the reverse path — Watcher context *supporting* a manual entry — is an INFERRED matter, §2.7). When a manual entry contradicts the OBSERVED record for the same window, the Life Log shows both, side by side, with their grades. No reconciliation prompt, no "did you mean…", no scoring of honesty.
- `[P0]` **P0 has no grades at all — and that is the honest P0 answer.** Spark ships the blank-page journal (write-only) and the manual five-state fire: no auto-capture exists, so no OBSERVED class exists, so nothing can pretend to be graded. The v6 claim "the log never lies" is trivially true in P0 because the log holds only what the user wrote. The grade system arrives exactly when the Watcher does (P1) — not before. This ordering is a design decision, not a limitation: BU's first contact with evidence grades should be a user who has already spent weeks writing their own record, so grades arrive as *labels on a familiar timeline*, not as a surveillance audit.
- `[all]` **The grade is never hidden from the person it describes.** Every export, every Forge sentence, every Life Log view carries the grade of every claim it uses (v6: "you always know what the app saw vs what you said vs what it inferred"). A pattern sentence built on USER_CONFIRMED entries says so.

### §2.2 · Why Self-Trackers Lapse — The Personal Informatics Literature

**Mechanism 1 — the stage model.** Li, Dey & Forlizzi (2010, *CHI '10*, pp. 557–566, doi:10.1145/1753326.1753409) derived, from surveys and interviews with people who collect and reflect on personal information, a **stage-based model of personal informatics systems** with **five stages**: **preparation** (deciding what and why to collect), **collection**, **integration** (preparing and connecting the collected data), **reflection**, and **action**. Four properties matter as much as the stages: (1) **barriers cascade** — problems at collection (effort, forgetting) poison integration and reflection downstream; (2) stages are **iterative** — people loop back; (3) each stage is **user-driven and/or system-driven** — design must balance automation and control; (4) systems are **uni-faceted or multi-faceted** — most tools capture one facet of life and thereby miss the connections. Their four design recommendations, verbatim from the abstract: systems should (1) "be designed in a holistic manner across the stages"; (2) "allow iteration between stages"; (3) "apply an appropriate balance of automated technology and user control within each stage"; (4) "explore support for associating multiple facets of people's lives" `[T1-venue; foundational; 1,700+ citations]`.

**Mechanism 2 — the lived model.** Epstein, Ping, Fogarty & Munson (2015, *UbiComp '15*, pp. 731–742, doi:10.1145/2750858.2804250) extended the stage model into a **lived-informatics model** built from three surveys of past and present self-trackers (105, 99, and 83 participants) `[T1-venue; sample composition detail page-level debt-listed]`. The lived model adds what the stage model excluded: the *attitudinal phases around* the loop — **deciding to track**, **selecting a tool**, and **tracking and acting** as a sustained, interruptible practice in which the Li-model stages (collection, integration, reflection, action) recur and break down over time. Its significance for BU is the unit it introduces: the *lapse* — a period where a still-installed tracker is no longer fed — as the normal state of self-tracking, not a failure state that design must prevent. The field map (Epstein, Caldeira, Figueiredo, Lu, Silva, Williams, Lee, Li et al. 2020, *IMWUT* 4(1), doi:10.1145/3432231) consolidates both models and confirms where the literature locates the pain: integration burden (getting data into one place), reflection difficulty (knowing what to look at), and the decision-to-track phases that determine whether any of it ever starts `[T2-venue map; record-level this pass]`.

**Mechanism 3 — what practitioners actually do.** Choe, Lee, Pratt & Kientz (2014, *CHI '14*, pp. 1143–1152, doi:10.1145/2556288.2557372) studied quantified-selfers' practices: tracking is negotiated continuously (what to track, when to stop), data collection competes with daily life, and even enthusiasts develop rituals to lower the cost — habitual slots, minimal entry formats, acceptance of gaps `[T1-venue; record-level this pass]`. Bridged from Phase 3: Wood, Quinn & Kashy (2002, *JPSP* 83:1281–1297) — nearly half of daily behavior is repeated in the same location almost daily and runs under context control — is the reason a *context-cued* log (place, anchor, time-of-day) is more than metadata: context fields are what make the Life Log's manual entries cheap to write and comparable across days.

**The design synthesis — a friction budget, not a retention system.** The personal-informatics literature converges on a mechanism BU's engagement data (Phase 5 D3) predicts independently: **manual-entry burden is the cascade's source**, and everything downstream (integration, reflection, the fire's honesty) inherits it. BU's countermeasure is arithmetic, not motivational:

- `[P0]` **The P0 answer: the Life Log does not exist in P0, and the journal is write-only.** Blank page, one surface, zero fields, zero grades, zero back-filling prompts. The only daily cost is the Evening Close's question set (Phase 1 D2). The friction budget for Spark is therefore *already met by construction* — and the build must not spend it: no optional "log your day" surface added to P0, however cheap it looks. Every manual field added before the Watcher exists competes with the two-touch ritual (Play + Close) for the same 30-second budget, and the ritual is the load-bearing member (Phase 5 D3).
- `[P1]` **Auto-capture first, manual always optional, gaps never nag.** The Watcher fills collection/integration without user work (Li's recommendation 3, the automation side). Manual off-phone entries (title + duration, per v6 §9) carry a hard friction budget — **≤30 s/day of required manual work** (declared, set-and-tested: the personal-informatics literature constrains the *existence* of the budget and its cascade role, not its exact seconds). The Life Log renders days with no manual entries as *gaps, marked "not tracked"*, never as zeros and never as a to-do.
- `[P1]` **The gap-handling flow ("what were you doing 2pm–5pm?"): one question, asked at most once per day, inside Evening Close, only if a ≥2 h gap exists, answer optional, one tap to skip, answer graded USER_CONFIRMED.** No same-day push about gaps (Phase 4 D3's two-class notification policy already forbids it); no streak of "logging completeness"; no "your record is incomplete" copy. The v6 ambition — "a complete record of your life" — is amended here: BU's record is *complete by evidence-graded construction* (every block is an entry, a gap-marker, or the user's account), never complete by coverage, because chasing coverage is exactly the cascade Li et al. documented.
- `[P1]` **Stage mapping as an architecture rule:** Watcher = collection/integration (system-driven); Evening Close = reflection (user-driven, protected — never automated, never summarized *at* the user before they have written); the Play = action. BU deliberately does *not* build a "reflection feed" (auto-generated dashboard insights) in P1 — that is the reflection stage colonized by the system, which the stage model predicts will atrophy the user-driven loop the Evening Close depends on. Forge patterns surface only in Sunday Review (P3, §2.6) and only after the user's own weekly write.
- `[P4]` **Multi-faceted association is the long game** (Li's recommendation 4): the Life Log's value compounds as facets connect (sleep line ↔ next-day fire ↔ drift ↔ place fields). The schema (D2) therefore carries context fields from day one — cheap to leave empty, expensive to retrofit years later.

### §2.3 · The Quantification Paradox — When Measuring Steals the Thing Measured

**Mechanism.** Etkin (2016, *Journal of Consumer Research* 42(6):967–984, doi:10.1093/jcr/ucv095) ran **six experiments** on what happens when an activity is measured. The two-sided finding, verbatim from the paper: "while measurement increases how much of an activity people do (e.g., walk or read more), it can simultaneously reduce how much people enjoy those activities." The causal path she documents: "measurement can undermine intrinsic motivation. By drawing attention to output, measurement can make enjoyable activities feel more like work, which reduces their enjoyment. As a result, measurement can decrease continued engagement in the activity and subjective well-being" `[T1; six studies, single lab program — flag: [REPLICATED-within-program, single-lab]; the mechanism is a measurement-side analogue of the crowding-out literature BU already uses (Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999, *Psych Bulletin*, bridged from Phase 1), which raises confidence; independent cross-lab replication is a research-debt item]`. The quantity being measured does not need a reward attached — "even in the absence of explicit external incentives, measurement itself can thus have similar effects." Attention to output is the mediating mechanism: the step count turns a walk into work product.

This is the measurement-side twin of Phase 1's reward finding, and it has the same shape: an intervention that reliably increases *frequency* while corroding the *reason* the behavior was worth doing. For a life-management app, that is the difference between a user who logs themselves into misery and one who uses the record to live better. The paradox also cuts in BU's favor in one specific place: activities BU *wants* the user to do more of and whose enjoyment BU is not protecting (studying for an exam, the drift-reduction target) are the safe zone for quantification. The danger zone is everything the user does *for its own sake*.

**What this means for BU — the never-quantify list.**

- `[P0]` **The blank page stays blind.** BU never counts words, entries, streaks, or sentiment in the journal. No word-count display, no "you wrote 4 days this week" line, no tone analysis. The journal is where Etkin's mechanism would bite hardest (writing *is* the intrinsic act), and its entire value model (v6: "Your reflection is data, not archive") depends on the user not writing *for* the measurement. The Forge (P3) reads journal entries on-device to *support* them (extract promises, link embers, resurface) — it never reports on their quantity or quality.
- `[P0]` **The fire is five states, never a number.** Phase 1/3/5 decisions converge here from a second direction: a percentage fire would quantify the very act of tending, and Etkin's mechanism predicts the state display would start to feel like a score to defend. The five-state manual fire with "MANUAL" honesty, the 7-day recomputation window, and the display-less Gorgeous gate (Phase 5 D4) are all measurement-hygiene decisions as much as reward-hygiene ones.
- `[P0]` **The Evening Close asks *what*, never *how much*, about experiences.** "What was today's best moment?" (§2.4) — a moment request. Never "rate your day 1–10", never "how productive was your day", never mood averages. The optional reflection line is unmeasured narrative.
- `[P0]` **Rest stays coarse.** Phase 3 D3's sleep line (hours-slept + rested-tap) enters as a *play-sizing prior*, displayed as a band, never as a sleep score, never trend-charted, never optimized ("your best sleep week yet!" banned — Phase 3 D9's sleep-scolding ban extends to sleep-praising). Rest is on the never-quantify list *as an experience*; the coarse band survives because it is functional input, not feedback — the distinction is stated in D7 so the boundary survives P2–P3 growth.
- `[P3]` **Relationships: tendings are events, not metrics.** "Tend Aisha" is a verified tending — an event the user chose to log, graded like any tending. BU never adds contact counts, never charts "relationship minutes", never compares ("you tended Aisha more than Rahul this month" is a permanent abstention, joining Phase 5 D6's comparison ban). The moment the fire's per-person tally becomes visible, tending people starts feeling like tending a leaderboard.
- `[P3]` **The drift report is the boundary case, handled by Phase 4 D6.** Drift minutes *are* quantified — and that is allowed because drift is the behavior BU is engineered to reduce, the report is the intervention, and the copy rules (report, never diagnose; no percentages; no fire pricing) keep it from becoming a score. But the same protection does not extend to what the user does *inside* non-drift leisure: the Watcher's app-class totals for whitelisted/neutral apps are recorded, never surfaced as verdicts about "relaxing right."

### §2.4 · Memory at Evening Close — Peak, End, and the Same-Day Horizon

**Mechanism 1 — peak-end and duration neglect.** Retrospective judgments of extended experiences do not integrate the experience; they compute a small summary from salient samples. Redelmeier & Kahneman (1996, *Pain* 66(1):3–8, doi:10.1016/0304-3959(96)02994-6) recorded real-time pain during **colonoscopy (n = 154)** and **lithotripsy (n = 133)** and compared the moment-by-moment record to each patient's retrospective judgment of "total pain." From the abstract: patients' judgments "were strongly correlated with the peak intensity of pain (P < 0.005) and with the intensity of pain recorded during the last 3 min of the procedure (P < 0.005). Despite substantial variation in the duration of the experience, lengthy procedures were not remembered as particularly aversive" — memories "largely reflect the intensity of pain at the worst part and at the final part of the experience" `[REPLICATED as a paradigm across stimuli and modalities; T1]`. The experimental core is older and sharper: Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber & Redelmeier (1993, *Psychological Science* 4:401–405) — the cold-pressor studies in which participants *preferred to repeat* a longer episode that added a warmer tail ("When More Pain Is Preferred to Less: Adding a Better End"), and Fredrickson & Kahneman (1993, *JPSP* 65:45–55) — **duration neglect** in retrospective evaluations of affective episodes `[both T1; record-level this pass]`.

**Mechanism 2 — structured same-day reconstruction works.** The Day Reconstruction Method (Kahneman, Krueger, Schkade, Schwarz & Stone 2004, *Science* 306(5702):1776–1780, doi:10.1126/science.1103572) combines time-budget measurement with experience sampling: participants "systematically reconstruct their activities and experiences of the preceding day with procedures designed to reduce recall biases." Its validation: "close correspondences between the DRM reports of **909 employed women** and established results from experience sampling" `[T1; replicated]`. The direct same-day comparison — Dockray, Grant, Stone, Kahneman, Wardle & Steptoe (2010, *Social Indicators Research*, doi:10.1007/s11205-010-9578-7) — monitored **94 working women** with six real-time EMA affect ratings per day plus a DRM reconstruction at day's end: "similar profiles of affect intensity were recorded with the two techniques," with attenuation-adjusted between-person correlations from **0.58** (stress, working day) to **0.90** (happiness, leisure day), unrelated to age, education, or depressed mood — the DRM "provides reasonably reliable estimates both of the intensity of affect and variations in affect over the day" `[REPLICATED; T1 — DRM–EMA correspondence replicated across independent samples (2004 discovery + 2010 validation)]`.

**What this means for BU.**

- `[P0]` **The 21:00 same-evening horizon is the evidence-backed instrument choice.** The recall gap BU asks the user to bridge at Evening Close is hours, not weeks — the regime where the DRM validation lives (same-day reconstruction matched EMA profiles; the attenuation-adjusted correlations of 0.58–0.90 are the honest accuracy band for *affect*, and behavior counts are trivially better when the count is small and recent). Next-morning closes (the classic failure mode when the 21:00 notification is missed) would double the gap and push the user from reconstruction toward schematic recall — the relight path therefore *permits* a late close but never schedules one (Phase 4 D3: fire-once, never repeat; Phase 1 D5: one tap + one play, not a backlog).
- `[P0]` **Question wording follows the summary mechanism.** Since retrospective judgment samples peaks and ends, the Evening Close should *ask for the samples the memory will keep anyway* and let them stand as the record: "What was today's best moment?" (peak request), "What was the last thing you tended?" (end request), and verified small counts (plays done — checked against the Play itself, so recall error is caught, not silently stored). What the Close must never ask: aggregate quality judgments ("how good was today, 1–10") — the peak-end mechanism guarantees such numbers are summaries *pretending to be measurements*, and the fake precision would flow into the Life Log as if it were graded data. If the user volunteers an evaluation in prose, it is stored as prose, USER_CONFIRMED, never parsed into a number.
- `[P0]` **"How was your day" questions never feed the fire.** The fire is computed from tendings and (later) verified events only — affect ratings, even honest ones, never brighten or dim it (this closes the loophole where a user could feel better about a drifted day by reporting a good mood, and the inverse manipulation where the app punishes honest bad moods).
- `[P3]` **The v6 memory-showcase ("see your life back, in detail, years later") inherits these bounds.** The Life Log's year-view is honest about its construction: days are *reconstructions with known instruments* — same-evening accounts plus auto-capture plus gap-markers — not recordings. Copy for the multi-year view (P4) must not promise "everything you did"; it promises what each grade can support, per D1's propagation rules.

### §2.5 · Predicting Honestly — Sequence Baselines, Conformal Intervals, and the Confidence-Language Table

**Mechanism 1 — the model class.** BU's prediction target (v6): "34% chance you do this tomorrow. Here's what raises it to 71%: start before noon." — tomorrow's tending probability for a play, from the user's own history. The honest baseline family is the one the located literature uses for next-action prediction: sequence models over app/behavior events. Katsarou, Yu & Beierle (2022, *IEEE Access* 10:18233–18247, doi:10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3150874) — **WhatsNextApp**, an LSTM-based next-app predictor evaluated against sequence baselines — is this pass's located representative of that literature `[T1-venue; single study; the class is broad, one representative is enough for a baseline spec, not for effect claims]`. BU's P3 baseline is deliberately *dumber* than the literature's state of the art: an **order-1 Markov (bigram) model over tending states** (did/didn't, by play and day-of-week) with simple recency and context features (window, place, sleep band) — because N=1 data is small, non-stationary, and the failure mode that matters is not model ceiling but *honesty about model floor*. An LSTM on 60 days of one person's tendings would memorize noise; a bigram model knows what it doesn't know.

**Mechanism 2 — the interval wrapper.** Angelopoulos & Bates (2021, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification," arXiv:2107.07511, v1 15 Jul 2021 / v6 7 Dec 2022) provide the standard construction: split conformal prediction holds out a calibration set, computes nonconformity scores (here: |predicted − actual| of the tending-probability model), and takes the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)/n⌉ quantile as the interval radius; the resulting prediction intervals carry "explicit, non-asymptotic guarantees even without distributional assumptions or model assumptions," containing the truth "with a user-specified probability, such as 90%" `[T2-venue tutorial (NeurIPS-crosslisted class); split-conformal recipe and exchangeability caveat cited from the tutorial, page-level check debt-listed]`. Two caveats are load-bearing for BU, both from the tutorial's own framing: the guarantee is **marginal** — it averages over possible users/draws, not "90% for this specific Tuesday" (conditional coverage is *not* guaranteed); and it requires **exchangeability** between calibration and future data — which BU's time series violates by construction (behavior drifts, Phase 5 D3). BU therefore uses *rolling* calibration windows (recent-only residuals), re-splits daily, and treats the conformal width as a *drift alarm*: intervals that suddenly widen are the model saying "I no longer know" — which triggers abstention, not a shrunk claim.

**Mechanism 3 — why to show uncertainty at all.** Joslyn & LeClerc (2012, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied* 18(2):126–140, doi:10.1037/a0025185) tested forecast formats in three experiments (road-salting decisions): "uncertainty information improved decision quality overall and increased trust in the forecast"; participants with uncertainty forecasts "took appropriate precautionary action and withheld unnecessary action more often," and when forecast error grew, the harm of deterministic forecasts was "attenuated by uncertainty forecasts"; categorical decision advice alone did not improve decisions `[T1; three experiments, one task domain]`. The transfer to BU: interval-bearing predictions are not just honest, they *work better for the recipient* — the user who sees "1 in 3, roughly" makes a better plan than the user who sees "34%."

**What this means for BU — the prediction output spec (D3's science half).**

- `[P3]` **Model stack, frozen:** bigram tending model + recency/context features → probability estimate → split-conformal interval from a 28-day rolling calibration window → confidence-language table → display. No black-box upgrade path without re-passing this phase's honesty gates.
- `[P3]` **Abstention rules (the model may refuse):** fewer than 21 calibrated days on the play; conformal width > 40 points; calibration drift alarm (residual distribution shift vs. the prior window); or the prediction's interval is uninformative (contains both "very likely" and "very unlikely"). Abstention copy: "Not enough of your own history to predict this yet." — never a fake number.
- `[P3]` **Percentages ship only inside intervals, translated to frequency language.** "34%" alone is banned; the display form is "On days like this, you've tended about 1 in 3" (a true empirical frequency) or the interval form "34% (roughly a quarter to a half)." No decimals below 5%, no pseudo-precision ("34.2%" banned — v6's "34%" is already at the precision floor and only survives because it rounds from a real count).
- `[P3]` **The counterfactual clause is banned in v1 of prediction and handled by the causal grammar (§2.6).** "Here's what raises it to 71%: start before noon" asserts a causal uplift that a bigram model cannot support — it is an *observational* contrast (you tended more on before-noon days) wearing a causal costume. The D4 grammar rewrites it as the contrast it actually is: "On days you started before noon, you tended 6 of 8. On later days, 2 of 9." — frequencies first, mechanism never claimed.
- `[P1]` **The confidence-language table ships with Forge v1's fire computation, not at P3** — because Forge v1 already speaks in tendencies (Phase 3 D4's causal-humble sleep grammar). The table (D3) assigns each claim class its display form: raw frequency counts ("4 of last 7") allowed anywhere; percentages only with intervals; causal verbs ("raises", "because", "leads to") nowhere outside a user-declared experiment (§2.6).

### §2.6 · Correlation vs. Causation in Forge Speech — Single-Case Designs, ITS, and the Enforced Grammar

**Mechanism 1 — what counts as within-user causal evidence.** BU's Forge makes sentences about one person's data ("You miss after sleeping under 6h"). The methodological literature that governs exactly this situation is the **single-case experimental design (SCED)** tradition, standardized for evidence review by the What Works Clearinghouse panel: Kratochwill, Hitchcock, Horner, Levin, Odom, Rindskopf & Shadish (2010, WWC Single-Case Design Technical Documentation, IES; and 2013, *Remedial and Special Education* 34(1):26–38, doi:10.1177/0741932512452794). The standards name the designs that can support causal claims within a case — **withdrawal/reversal (ABAB), multiple baseline, alternating treatments, changing criterion** — and grade them **Meets Standards / Meets Standards with Reservations / Does Not Meet**, checking per-phase data quality (stability of baseline, number of data points per phase — current versions ask ≥5 per phase, version detail debt-listed), operationalized outcomes, and inter-assessor agreement `[T2-grade standards; the 2023 update (Kratochwill et al., *Journal of School Psychology*, doi:10.1016/j.jsp.2022.12.002) extends rather than overturns them]`. The two properties that make a SCED causal, distilled for BU: **systematic manipulation** (the putative cause is turned on/off/by design, not merely watched) and **within-phase stability** (the outcome isn't already swinging).

**Mechanism 2 — the observational time series.** When there is no manipulation — most of Forge's material — the honest tool is **interrupted time series (ITS)** analysis: Bernal, Cummins & Gasparrini (2016/2017, *International Journal of Epidemiology*, doi:10.1093/ije/dyw098) give the segmented-regression tutorial — model the outcome as level + trend, add the intervention indicator and its interaction with time, **propose the impact model a priori**, and handle the series' own pathologies (autocorrelation, overdispersion, seasonality, time-varying confounders) before believing a change point `[T2-venue tutorial; record-level this pass; vol/pages debt-listed]`. ITS converts "something changed when X happened" into "the level/slope changed by an estimated amount, with uncertainty, conditional on the model and the absence of competing changes." Its a-priori requirement is the transferable discipline: BU may only examine a change-point when the change was *declared* (a new window, a new whitelist, a stress window) — never by scanning for whatever pattern fits.

**Mechanism 3 — the grammar as enforcement.** The gap between what the data supports (co-occurrence) and what the sentence asserts (cause) is closable by vocabulary policy: Phase 3 D4 already built the causal-humble grammar for sleep claims; this phase generalizes it into an enforced Forge-wide grammar with compile-time rules (D4). The v6 pattern sentences fail it in instructive ways — "You miss after sleeping under 6h" asserts cause by temporal framing ("after"); "You nail it when the deadline is <3 days out" is a frequency fact wearing a performance verdict ("nail"); "The morning play matters more than you think" is causal commentary on a conditional frequency. Each has a pass-form rewrite in D4.

**What this means for BU.**

- `[P3]` **Three claim classes, three evidence bars** (D4): (1) *frequency claims* ("you tended 4 of last 7") — any grade, always allowed, always true counts; (2) *co-occurrence claims* ("on days you studied before noon, you tended Aisha that evening 78% of the time") — require a minimum cell size (n ≥ 5 in the smaller cell, set-and-tested), association verbs only, and the base rates shown alongside; (3) *change claims* ("since you moved study to mornings, your weekly tendings went 2 → 4") — require a user-declared change point, a minimum 14-day post-period, a segmented comparison against the pre-period trend, and no competing declared changes in window. Anything that would need manipulation the user didn't perform is not a claim BU makes — it is an experiment BU offers (D5).
- `[P3]` **Confounds that silently fabricate patterns** get named in the Forge's internal check list: regression to the mean (patterns flagged after extreme days are usually the mean returning), selection (comparing tended days to untended days compares good days to bad days by construction), shared-cause co-occurrence (morning study and evening Aisha-tending co-move because *good days* cause both), and confirmation sampling (Sunday Review surfaces patterns the user's priors prefer). The Forge's claim formatter must state the class of its evidence ("in your last 60 days…"), never simulate mechanism.
- `[P3]` **"Causal chains" (v6 §6) pass or die at the grammar.** The v6 example is retained only in its pass-form: "When you tend Calculus in the morning, you tend Aisha that evening 78% of the time. BU doesn't know why." — the last sentence is not humility theater; it is the grammar's required companion to every conditional frequency.

### §2.7 · Evidence Grades and Data Quality — Operational Criteria for the Four Grades

**Mechanism.** Data-quality research supplies the conceptual foundation the grades need: quality is **fitness for use**, and it must be anchored in the difference between the real-world system (the day, as lived) and the information system that represents it (Wand & Wang 1996, *Communications of the ACM* 39(2):86–95, doi:10.1145/240455.240479 — data-quality dimensions fail in practice because representations are treated as if they were the reality they stand for) `[T1-venue; foundational; record-level this pass]`. A grade is a *provenance label* stating how far a stored entry's representation sits from the event it claims to represent. The v6 doc defines the four grades (verbatim): auto-captured phone events **OBSERVED**; manual entries **USER_CONFIRMED**; context-inferred entries ("you were probably in class because the phone was off 9am–12pm on a weekday") **INFERRED, marked as such**; outcome-verified entries ("exam score proves the study happened"; the photo-embedding novelty check) **OUTCOME_CONFIRMED** — "The grades are visible in the log — you always know what the app saw vs what you said vs what it inferred."

What the v6 doc does not supply is the *operational* layer: what each grade requires to be assigned, what downgrades it, and how it propagates into the fire. That layer is D1's deliverable; its criteria are set here.

**What this means for BU.**

- `[P1]` **Grade assignment is by acquisition channel, never by plausibility** (D1 table): OBSERVED requires Watcher-class system capture (UsageStats events, FGS log, seal/tending taps); USER_CONFIRMED requires a user-authored entry; INFERRED requires a *displayable rule* (the v6 school-hours example is a rule, and the rule must be shown with the entry); OUTCOME_CONFIRMED requires an external verification event (exam score logged and dated to the play; photo embedding passing the novelty check). No heuristic may *upgrade* a grade. Plausibility checks may only *downgrade to INFERRED-with-flag* (e.g., a manual entry whose time window contradicts the Watcher's record for the same window stays USER_CONFIRMED but is displayed adjacent to the contradicting OBSERVED record — §2.1).
- `[P1]` **Downgrade triggers are explicit and visible:** Watcher suspension (usage-access revoked, force-stopped, battery-kill) re-grades the affected stream's future as *gap-marked* (nothing is inferred over a dead sensor — Phase 4 D1's never-counts and grace logic already protect the drift computation; D1 extends it to the record); clock-skew or timestamp anomalies mark entries suspect, never silently corrected; the photo-embedding path deletes the raw photo after embedding (v6) and the derived OUTCOME_CONFIRMED tending cites "photo-verified," with the embedding retained.
- `[P1]` **Propagation into the fire (seed, resolved in Phase 7):** weights OUTCOME_CONFIRMED 1.0 · OBSERVED 0.9 · USER_CONFIRMED 0.5 · INFERRED 0.3 over the 7-day decaying window (Phase 7's seed function, justified or replaced there). This phase adds two constraints the weights alone don't carry: **INFERRED entries never trigger a fire-state transition on their own** (a transition requires at least one non-INFERRED tending in the window — Card B), and **Gorgeous-gate eligibility counts only verified tendings** (OBSERVED/OUTCOME_CONFIRMED; the gate is "30-day verified-tending streak," so self-reported tendings cannot carry it).
- `[P1]` **Grades survive export** (v6 Bedrock invariant: full Markdown export, no account, no cloud): export format carries grade tags per entry (`[observed]`/`[user]`/`[inferred]`/`[outcome]`/`[gap]`). A grade that disappears in export is a grade that didn't exist.
- `[all]` **The v6 comparison table's "5 grades (SCHEDULED → OUTCOME_CONFIRMED)" line is an inconsistency, amended here:** BU has **four evidence grades**. "Scheduled" is an entry *status* (planned, awaiting evidence) in the Life Log, never an evidence grade — a scheduled play that didn't happen is a gap-marker, not a data point. Logged as a v6-doc amendment (§5 of the master plan's tracker workflow).

### §2.8 · Privacy, Consent, and What Passive Data Can Say — Digital Phenotyping's Humility Lesson

**Mechanism 1 — privacy decisions are made under uncertainty, context-dependently, and manipulably.** Acquisti, Brandimarte & Loewenstein (2015, *Science* 347(6221):509–514, doi:10.1126/science.aaa1465) organize the empirical record into three themes, verbatim: "people's uncertainty about the consequences of privacy-related behaviors and their own preferences over those consequences; the context-dependence of people's concern, or lack thereof, about privacy; and the degree to which privacy concerns are malleable—manipulable by commercial and governmental interests" `[T2-grade review, T1 venue]`. For a consent moment, this predicts exactly where honest consent fails: the user cannot know the consequences (so they must be stated concretely), context shifts concern (so the moment's framing drives the decision more than the policy), and design details are themselves manipulations (so the flow must be audited like any dark-pattern surface — Phase 5 D7's audit list applies).

**Mechanism 2 — consent fatigue is real and its cost is *less* engagement with the decision.** Choi, Park & Jung (2018, *Computers in Human Behavior* 81:42–51, doi:10.1016/j.chb.2017.12.001) operationalize **privacy fatigue** as emotional exhaustion plus cynicism about privacy decision-making, and find it "significantly affect[s] online privacy behaviors," with fatigue predicting disengagement (giving up on protective behavior) more strongly than privacy concern predicts protection `[T1; single study; survey-based, page-level debt-listed]`. The consent design implication is counterintuitive but well-supported across the fatigue and debiasing literatures: fewer, more meaningful consent moments beat many small ones; a consent that ends in exhaustion ends in a yes that means nothing.

**Mechanism 3 — digital phenotyping sets the honest ceiling for the Watcher's claims.** Onnela & Rauch (2016, *Neuropsychopharmacology* 41(7):1691–1696, doi:10.1038/npp.2016.7) define the practice: phone use generates "a surprisingly rich tapestry of social and behavioral fingerprints," which, because they reflect "the lived experiences of people in their natural environments, with granular temporal resolution," may support "precise and temporally dynamic disease phenotypes" `[T1; position paper]`. The Torous, Onnela & Keshavan line (Torous, Onnela & Keshavan 2017, *Translational Psychiatry* 7:e1053, doi:10.1038/tp.2017.25) frames phenotyping within RDoC and catalogs the passive streams in active psychiatric research — GPS, accelerometer, voice samples, call/text logs, screen-use data — e.g., smartphone streams collected "up to 1 million data points per day per subject" in schizophrenia cohorts `[T1; emerging clinical science]`. What that literature *does not* claim is the part BU must inherit: passive streams are *research instruments under validation* — signals whose clinical and behavioral meanings are established study-by-study, stream-by-stream, mostly in clinical populations and mostly not yet `[EMERGING]`. A phone-off interval can mean class, sleep, dead battery, a movie, or a phone the user lent out — the v6's own INFERRED example works only because it states its rule and its uncertainty.

**What this means for BU.**

- `[P1]` **The "Light the fire" consent moment is rebuilt as an informed moment (D6).** The v6 prompt ("Download the local model (529MB)? This activates the Watcher — a persistent service that sees what apps you use and when. Everything stays on your device.") is honest in direction but incomplete in the ways Acquisti et al. predict. D6 adds the five facts the user actually needs: **what is collected** (app identity class, timestamps, durations — never message content, never screenshots), **what stays on device** (everything, including the 529 MB model — no account, no cloud, no telemetry), **what it costs** (storage + the v6's own ~3–5%/day battery figure, verbatim from v6 §12), **how to stop** (one toggle; the Watcher stops; BU records the gap and nothing else changes), and **what the user keeps regardless** (the journal, the fire's history, full export — Phase 5 D7's invariant). One screen, five facts, two buttons, no pre-selected defaults, no urgency, no dismissal shaming — the same audit list Phase 5 D7 applied to the goes-dark mechanic applies here.
- `[P1]` **Consent is once, granular later, revocable always.** Against consent fatigue: no re-asking after updates for unchanged scope, no per-feature consent sprinkled through onboarding, later granularity as *settings the user can tighten* (per-stream toggles at P2), and the explicit note that declining Full mode routes to Lite without loss of Lite's value (v6's two-tier structure already guarantees this — the consent cannot be a wall between the user and the journal).
- `[P1]` **Watcher interpretation caps (D7) inherit phenotyping's humility:** the Watcher reports app-class counts, timestamps, drift minutes — *trends*, never states. No "you seem anxious", no "addicted" language (Phase 4 D6's ban-list already holds), no inference from phone-off to sleep beyond Phase 3 D4's ±45-min-class bounds-and-trend language, no psychological labels from mobility or social-app patterns even where the research literature is exploring them — because the research literature itself flags that work as `[EMERGING]`.
- `[P0]` **The P0 answer is architectural:** Spark has no Watcher, no model download, no consent moment to get wrong. The only data BU P0 holds is what the user typed, stored on device, exported on demand. This is worth stating as a build rule, not a consolation: the P0 journal must be implemented from day one as *exportable, plain-file, no-account local storage* — so that P1's consent conversation happens about added value, not about retroactive access to the user's existing words.

## 4 · Contradiction Cards

**Card A — Quantify the doing, or protect the enjoying?** *Side 1:* progress monitoring moves behavior (Harkin et al. 2016 meta, d ≈ .40, bridged from Phase 1) — self-monitoring *is* the Evening Close's engine, and quantified feedback is BU's core loop. *Side 2:* measurement corrodes enjoyment of the measured activity and continued engagement with it (Etkin 2016, six experiments), and the corroded activities are precisely the intrinsic ones. *Actually disputed:* not whether both effects exist but *where the boundary sits* — Etkin's own studies show output gains; the dispute is whether the enjoyment cost transfers from lab tasks (walking, reading) to the activities BU measures (studying, tending people). *Working resolution (D7):* BU quantifies *behaviors* (tendings, drift minutes, verified events) and refuses to quantify *experiences* (journal, mood, rest quality, relationships-as-metrics) — the boundary is the activity's purpose, not the measurement's convenience. The one place the line is genuinely blurred — drift minutes priced against leisure apps — is held inside Phase 4 D6's report-only copy rules. *Flip condition:* evidence that behavioral self-monitoring at BU's intensity degrades enjoyment of the *target* activities themselves (studying) would force the monitoring loop to thin (counts only, no trends).

**Card B — Should INFERRED entries earn fire weight at all?** *Side 1:* the master plan's Phase 7 seed gives INFERRED 0.3; dropping it discards the Watcher's inferential value and leaves INFERRED as decoration. *Side 2:* INFERRED is built on exactly the passive-interpretation leap the phenotyping literature warns about (§2.8) — an inference from a rule the user didn't write shouldn't add heat to a fire the user has to be able to trust. *Actually disputed:* whether rule-based inferences (phone-off 9–12 on a weekday) are epistemically closer to OBSERVED (a deterministic rule over OBSERVED inputs) or to speculation (the rule's premise fits many lives). *Working resolution (D1):* INFERRED keeps the 0.3 seed *but* never triggers a fire-state transition alone, never counts toward the Gorgeous gate, and always displays its rule — so the fire's *state* (the thing the user acts on) is never driven by anything the app merely guessed; INFERRED only shades the *coaching layer's* picture. *Flip condition:* if N=1 grade-agreement data (how often INFERRED rules match the user's account, measurable via the gap-flow) shows ≥90% agreement over 60 days, the weight cap is revisited in Phase 7 with that number in hand.

**Card C — How far may passive data be interpreted?** *Side 1:* the digital-phenotyping program demonstrates real signal in passive streams — granular, longitudinal, naturalistic (Onnela & Rauch 2016); refusing all interpretation wastes BU's distinctive capability. *Side 2:* the same literature marks those signals as research-stage, population-level, and clinically framed `[EMERGING]` — a consumer app that turns app-class counts into behavioral verdicts is exceeding what the evidence supports, and Phase 4 D6 already had to ban the copy that crept in. *Actually disputed:* not the data's existence but its *interpretive latency* — how much meaning can be extracted before validation catches up. *Working resolution (D7):* the Watcher reports *its own counts* (app-class, duration, drift minutes — the things UsageStats actually measures) and nothing above them: no states, no traits, no mood, no prediction of behavior beyond the D3 pipeline that shows its work. The v6 Forge gets exactly one interpretive product — patterns and predictions under the D3/D4 grammar — and everything else is a mirror. *Flip condition:* a stream-specific validation literature for consumer-grade phone data (not clinical cohorts) that BU could cite per claim — none exists this pass; the research-debt line tracks it.
